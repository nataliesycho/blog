There's a moment in every project where you have to choose your tools. The
temptation is always to reach for the most powerful, most flexible, most
future-proof option available. I've learned to resist it.

Powerful tools have a hidden cost: they ask you to make decisions you don't
have enough information to make yet. A framework with a thousand options is a
thousand small forks in the road, and most of them don't matter.

## Constraints are a feature

When a tool does one thing, it does that thing well and gets out of your way.
You spend your attention on the work instead of on the tool. That's the whole
game — attention is the scarce resource, not capability.

A few things I now optimize for:

1. **Boring by default.** Proven, well-documented, unsurprising.
2. **Easy to leave.** If switching away is cheap, committing is cheap too.
3. **Fast feedback.** The shorter the loop between change and result, the
   better my judgment gets.

## The payoff

Simple tools don't just make the work easier — they make it *better*. When the
medium stops fighting you, the quality of your thinking becomes the only
variable left. That's a good place to be.

That's why this site is a few Markdown files and some CSS. Nothing to maintain,
nothing to break, nothing between me and the writing.
